## Pinned card headers and the accordion fold (`lib/fold-panes.ts`)

An expanded card pins its header 8px below the top of the message list, so a tall diff, output or reasoning chain can be folded away without scrolling back up to a header that has left the viewport. The structure carries the feature, following kilo/opencode's accordion (`.pin-card` in `app/globals.css`): the card's own box clips nothing and paints nothing, so the header's sticky containing block stays the message list; the header draws the top edge and the top radii and `.pin-card-body` the bottom ones (the outer radius is the card's 7px, so the fill's corner is 6px); both paint the card tint composited over `var(--bg)`, which keeps a pinned bar opaque; and `.pin-card::before` is the band over the pinned header — page background in a block that takes no net height, hides behind the header at rest, and covers the 8px gap plus the 6px the rounded corners leave uncovered once it pins. Nothing detects whether the header is stuck: there is no scroll listener and no re-render. The list's top inset lives on ChatWindow's wrapper rather than on the scroll container, so a sticky offset is measured from the visible top edge.

`foldPanes()` runs the panes' heights and the list's `scrollTop` from one rAF loop — a pinned header only *looks* pinned, so the list comes back by exactly what the panes give up. Both directions run the same pop: the stretch a reader can watch — the part of the panes' edge that crosses the viewport — gets a fixed 150ms (`POP_DURATION_MS`) with an ease-out quint, and the pixels beyond it, above the viewport or below, get time in proportion to that stretch, capped at `HIDDEN_MAX_MS` (150ms) as well; the run is therefore a hidden lead, the watched pop and a hidden trail, with the list's compensation following the same curve. The duration still does not scale with the distance — a card ten screens tall opens where it shows at the same speed as one that just fills the viewport, and neither spends a second moving nothing anyone can see — and an opening card and a closing one stay in step. Its easing is a quint rather than a cubic, so the panes are simply there once they land instead of creeping through the last stretch. Result images stay outside the `[data-pin-pane]` wrappers and therefore stay visible; reduced motion applies the compensation without the animation; a wheel or tap mid-fold stops the scroll writes and leaves the reader where they put themselves; and interrupting a fold gives the panes their own height back. `e2e/tool-card-sticky.mjs` covers the pinned offset, the paint and hit-testing of the room above the header, the fold, reduced motion, the interruption, and the auto-collapse that fires once the card has scrolled fully above the list.

Opening is the same loop run backwards. `foldPanes()` and `unfoldPanes()` are two thin wrappers over one `runFold()` that carries a direction, so a card that opens covers the ground the fold gave up instead of appearing. The unfold is a pop rather than a slide — the same watched 150ms and ease-out quint the fold runs, because the reader asked for this one and is waiting on it. Only the fold moves the list back: the panes it clears sit above the content below them, while an unfold grows them back under the header, where nothing above it moves — and a frame-by-frame absolute `scrollTop` would overwrite `collapseOffscreenPanes()`'s give-back for a sibling card that folds itself away mid-animation, pushing the card being opened out of the list's top edge for its own observer to collapse again. The panes an unfold has to open are mounted by the expand itself, so the click cannot measure them: it only records the intent — `expandedRef` tells an interrupt of a running fold, where the panes are on screen and the fold is simply reversed, from an open out of the collapsed state, where the body has yet to be committed — and a `useLayoutEffect` starts the run on the frame that body arrived, after the panes have been zeroed and before the browser paints. The unfold's `settle()` clears the panes' inline height rather than writing `"0px"`, so a thinking body that lands later, or a tool result that keeps streaming in, is not frozen at the height it happened to have mid-animation. `setOpen()` (the persisted `pi-thinking-expanded` preference) and `collapseOffscreenPanes()` (a card that scrolled off screen) both stay instant: neither is a state the reader asked for frame by frame. An interrupt finishes the running run first — it settles rather than being taken over half way — and the next one starts from that settled state, with the reopen re-asserting `setExpanded(true)` in the same batch: that settle queued the fold's own `setExpanded(false)`, and the render it lands in would otherwise unmount the body the unfold has just measured — a tool card's panes are gated on `expanded`, so the unfold would animate detached nodes while the chevron said the card was open. `e2e/tool-card-sticky.mjs` samples an opening card frame by frame, which is what holds that it pops open in its own short time, settles at the pane's natural height, and opens in a single frame under reduced motion.
A pane is capped at 70vh (`.pin-card-body [data-pin-pane]`), so a long command, diff or reasoning chain shows as a preview that fades out at its bottom (`data-clipped` is written on every render where the pane's `scrollHeight` exceeds its `clientHeight + 1`, with `mask-image` in `app/globals.css`) and a **Show all** row sits under it: one click puts the cap back to `max-height: none` (`.pin-card[data-full="true"]`, `chat.showAll` in all three locales), and a fold returns the card to the preview for the next open. The cap belongs on the pane, not on `.pin-card-body`: the fold drives each pane's own inline height and the list compensates by exactly that, so a capped body would leave the panes laid out at their full height and desynchronise the two. It bounds a *pane* rather than a card — four panes still outgrow the viewport, which is what their header pins against, and why `e2e/tool-card-sticky.mjs` asserts that the card outgrows the list rather than the pane. The Show-all row is a `[data-pin-pane]` itself, since the fold must hand its height back with the rest, and its padding and separator sit on an inner span: a zero-height box still shows its own padding, which left 13px behind and moved the message under the card by that much as the card collapsed.

An expanded card that scrolls fully above the list folds itself away. `usePinnedCard` observes the card with an `IntersectionObserver` rooted at the list's scroll container and, once the card's whole box — sticky header included — has passed above the root's top, `collapseOffscreenPanes()` zeroes the panes' heights and subtracts the same total from the list's `scrollTop` in one synchronous go, so whatever is still on screen does not move. The list's position is read *before* the panes shrink and written back as an absolute value: a read after the change forces the layout in which the browser's own scroll anchoring has already given the height back, and subtracting the same total a second time scrolled the reader twice as far — an expanded card leaving the top jumped by its own pane height. Because the write is absolute and lands on the value anchoring lands on, it is a no-op in Chrome and Firefox and stays the compensation in Safari, which has no scroll anchoring. It compensates by the panes' height rather than the header's displacement: once the card is off screen the header no longer travels with the scroll, and `foldPanes`'s `shift` would be 0 and the list would jump. The observer is mounted only while the card is open and is disconnected on unmount. The fold is viewport-only: it never calls `onToggle`, so the reader's persisted expansion preference is untouched. A `seen` gate means the card must have intersected the root at least once, so a thinking block opened by the expand-all preference below the initial scroll position is not collapsed before anyone has seen it.

The mechanism is shared rather than tool-specific. Both `ToolCallBlock` and `ThinkingCard` render `components/PinnedCard.tsx` and drive it with `hooks/usePinnedCard.ts` (the open/expanded/fold state machine); `.tool-card` and `.thinking-card` only set the tint over the shared `.pin-card*` structure, and panes use `[data-pin-pane]`. `ThinkingCard` lives in its own file, while `ThinkingBlock` in the upstream-synced `components/MessageView.tsx` is only a one-line delegate so that file's diff stays small; `lib/thinking-content.ts` holds the deferred-body fetch, and a thinking body is capped at `min(560px, 70vh)` so it scrolls inside the card like a tool result and never outgrows the pane's own cap on a short window. `e2e/thinking-card-sticky.mjs` covers the pinned offset, the cap and the fold.

`ToolCallBlock` is the one place in `MessageView.tsx` whose diff cannot stay small: upstream keeps rewriting its header, body and result rendering, so a sync has to fold their new pieces into the `PinnedCard` shell. Every foldable body goes in a `[data-pin-pane]` wrapper, `showsArgs` / `showsCodemode` / `showsPatch` / `showsResult` decide what mounts, and `hasBody` has to name each of them or the card renders nothing below its header. Result images stay unwrapped, exactly as upstream renders them.

## Shell call timeout badge

`lib/duration-format.ts` labels a whole number of seconds as `57s` / `1m 30s` / `2m` / `1h 5m`, plus `remainingTimeoutSeconds` clamped to `[0, timeout]`; `lib/tool-names.ts` adds `isShellToolName` and `getShellTimeout`. `components/ShellTimeoutBadge.tsx` counts the time a running `bash` / `powershell` call has left before its `timeout`, in the slot that later holds the elapsed duration, and `ToolCallBlock` in the upstream-synced `components/MessageView.tsx` only passes it that call's start time (the assistant message's `timestamp`, via `BlockView`) and keeps the elapsed-duration rendering.

The badge is fork-only and lives in its own file so `MessageView.tsx` gains no state, effect or interval — the countdown's 1s tick is there, stopped once it reaches 0. Pi streams a running shell tool's output as a partial result that carries no timestamp, and the elapsed duration is derived from that timestamp, so `!result?.timestamp` is what marks the call unfinished (upstream renders that duration as a plain `{duration}s`). The countdown starts from the message timestamp rather than the tool's real start, so it can reach 0 a few seconds before pi kills the command, which is why the zero state is the `chat.toolTimeoutEnding` key (`terminating soon` / `即将终止` / `即將終止`) in all three locales instead of a `0s`; the running value itself carries no words — `chat.toolTimeout` is gone. Badge text is the card's warning red (`#f87171`, the same colour an erroring tool name gets) in every state: the countdown, the full limit shown while the message still streams, and the zero state. `components/ThinkingCard.tsx` labels its own duration with the same formatter.

## ntfy notifications (`lib/ntfy.ts`)

The two upstream completion notifications are browser-bound: `lib/web-push.ts` needs a registered push subscription and `lib/browser-notifications.ts` needs the Notification permission and a hidden window, which is a high bar on a phone. `lib/ntfy.ts` adds a server-side runner instead: the user writes one `curl` command with `{{…}}` placeholders, and when a top-level session finishes Pi Web substitutes them and runs the command, so a phone subscribed to the resulting ntfy topic gets the message — no browser permission, no open window, no service worker.

Run, not interpret: `splitShellWords()` (upstream's `lib/shell-words.ts`, which already refuses pipes, `&&`, redirects and `$(…)`) turns the command into an argv list, the `{{…}}` substitution happens **after** the split so a title with spaces or quotes stays one argv word, and the first word must be `curl`. The process then runs with no shell and a timeout; a failure is logged as an exit code only, so a token in the command never reaches a log. The setting is deliberately just two fields — `enabled` and `command` — in the fork's own `~/.pi/agent/pi-web.json` under `ntfy`, written with the same read-modify-write as `defaultProjectPath`. `GET`/`PUT /api/ntfy` read and write it and `POST /api/ntfy` runs the stored command once with test values. Settings › General renders a command box, a row of tappable `{{…}}` chips, and a collapsible help that lists the placeholders and ready-made commands; on a wide panel the help sits beside the command, on a phone it stacks below (and the settings modal follows `--app-viewport-height`, so the keyboard does not cover the buttons). `lib/ntfy-templates.ts` (kept client-safe so the browser bundle never pulls in `node:child_process`) exports `NTFY_VARIABLES` and `NTFY_COMMAND_EXAMPLES`, which the chips, the help and the unit test all read, so the help cannot drift from the code. `{{url}}` comes from the optional `PI_WEB_PUBLIC_URL` environment variable. The trigger is one call next to upstream's push call in `lib/rpc-manager.ts`'s `onAgentRunComplete` — an upstream file, so the diff stays at one import and one call.

## Active project tabs

Parallel agents are the normal case: several projects can hold a running agent, or a finished run the user has not opened yet, at the same time. The sidebar's project dropdown already counted that activity (`getProjectActivity`), but seeing it meant opening the sidebar. The main top bar now renders one tab per project, running ones first, immediately left of the file-panel toggle — which is why that toggle narrowed from 36px to 28px (`FILE_PANEL_TOGGLE_SIZE` in `components/AppShell.tsx`; `TOP_BAR_ICON_BUTTON_SIZE` stays 36 for the remaining top-bar buttons).

`getProjectTabs()` in `lib/project-groups.ts` is the whole selector: `getRecentProjects()` (newest activity first, one row per stable project key) is stable-sorted by rank — a project with a running agent first, then one with an unread completion, then an idle project — and sliced to `Math.max(MIN_PROJECT_TABS, busy)`, so recency survives inside a rank and every busy project stays in the strip while idle projects top the list up to `MIN_PROJECT_TABS = 3`. That is why the strip almost always renders something: only a user with fewer than three projects, or none at all, sees fewer than three tabs. When the strip does render nothing the toggle takes back the right-aligned `auto` margin it always had. Each tab carries the project panel's own badge: `showProjectActivity` is exported from `components/SessionSidebar.tsx` and called with the `getProjectActivity` map plus a `compact` flag, which keeps only the spinning ring — so the 32px mobile chips show the same ring the sidebar dropdown shows without its counts.

## Bun terminal backend (bun-pty)

Bun cannot drive node-pty: the PTY exits immediately with code 1 and emits zero bytes, so the terminal tab stayed dead under `dev:bun` / `start:bun`. `lib/terminal-pty.ts` isolates the choice — `isBunRuntime()` reads a `Bun` global, `ptyModuleName(inBun, override)` returns `"bun-pty"` under Bun and `"node-pty"` under Node, `PI_WEB_TERMINAL_BACKEND=node-pty|bun-pty` overrides the guess (any other value means auto), and `loadPtyModule()` requires the chosen module inside `createTerminal`, so a native-load failure still reaches the API's JSON error path. `lib/terminal-manager.ts` only calls `loadPtyModule()`; nothing else in it changed, which keeps the upstream file's diff to an import plus that two-line load.

Both modules expose the same `spawn(file, args, options)` → `IPty` shape, so `PtyModule` / `PtySpawnOptions` structural types stand in for node-pty's own types and the adapter imports `IPty` as a type only. The two differ where it matters: bun-pty's `kill()` ignores the signal and fires `onExit` synchronously, so `killTerminal`'s SIGKILL escalation after two seconds is a second SIGTERM — harmless, because the process is already gone.

bun-pty goes in `optionalDependencies` (pinned `0.4.11`). It cannot go in `serverExternalPackages`: that list only externalizes `.mjs`, `.cjs`, `.js`, `.json` and `.node` entries, and bun-pty's package entry is a `.ts` file. Bundling it fails too — it imports `bun:ffi` and `dlopen`s a native library resolved at runtime, and Turbopack resolves even a literal `createRequire` specifier (`⨯ ./node_modules/bun-pty/src/index.ts` / `Error: Unknown module type` 500s every route). An `import`/`require` of `node:module` is not the way out either: in a production build the bundler swaps it for a module-map emulation that only resolves modules already in the graph, so `createRequire(__filename)("bun-pty")` works under `next dev` and then throws `Cannot find module 'bun-pty'` when `bin/pi-web.js` serves the `build:bun` output. `loadBunPty()` therefore takes the real `createRequire` from `process.getBuiltinModule("module")` — an ordinary property read no bundler rewrites — and resolves from `__filename` (the emitted server chunk), falling back to `process.cwd()` (the package directory `bin/pi-web.js` spawns `next start` in), so no static request naming bun-pty ever reaches a bundler. The prebuilt `librust_pty` shared library ships inside the tarball; `BUN_PTY_LIB` points at it when a platform build is missing, and `lib/terminal-pty.ts` names that variable — with the `bun add bun-pty` / `npm install bun-pty` fallback — in the error a failed load throws. `lib/terminal-pty.test.mjs` pins the selector; `e2e/terminal-bun.mjs` runs the whole terminal against a Bun dev server and asserts a real shell echo, exit code and close.

## Theme palettes and the derived dark list (`lib/theme.ts`)

Upstream names the dark palettes twice inside one file: `isDarkTheme()` compares `theme === "dark" || theme === "pine"`, and the injected `THEME_INIT_SCRIPT` repeats the same pair in its `r.classList.toggle("dark", ...)`. The fork makes the option list the single source instead. Every `THEME_OPTIONS` entry carries `resolvesTo: "light" | "dark" | "system"` — `"system"` being `auto`, which follows the OS scheme rather than naming a palette — and the tuple is pinned with `as const satisfies readonly ThemeOption[]`, so a new palette that omits the field fails `tsc` (TS2741) instead of silently rendering as light. `DARK_THEME_IDS: readonly ResolvedTheme[]` is filtered out of that list, and both `isDarkTheme()` and the first-paint script read it, so the script's toggle is generated (`r.classList.toggle("dark",["dark","pine"].includes(t))`) and the two can no longer drift; the emitted string is unchanged for the six current palettes.

A sync has to fold upstream's hardcoded pair back into the derived list rather than keep both: a palette that is dark in `THEME_OPTIONS` but missing from the script's list flashes light on the first paint, because the inline script sets `class="dark"` before hydration, and it is the class — not `data-theme` — that drives `globals.css`'s three `html.dark` rules (the Catppuccin file icons and the web-login error). Adding a palette stays four edits: the entry, its `[data-theme="…"]` block in `app/globals.css`, an SVG branch in `components/ThemeIcon.tsx` and a `settings.theme*` label in the three locales. `lib/theme.test.mjs` already loops over every `THEME_OPTIONS` id and compares the script's `html.dark` against `isDarkTheme()`, so it fails on a script left holding a stale list.

A sync will also bring back the dead top-bar theme menu: `e2e/themes.mjs`'s second half — the `Theme: …` button, the `Appearance` menu, its `menuitemradio` / Escape / dismissal / focus checks and its 1440px "dark tokens stay neutral gray" assertions — and the three locales' `theme.*` labels (`Theme: Light` … `Theme: System`) were removed here, because that UI no longer exists in this checkout (the Settings radio group is the live selector) and the second half could only time out. Delete them again after a sync. Note that the neutral-gray check has no replacement, so `dark`'s greys are not checked for a colour cast anywhere.
